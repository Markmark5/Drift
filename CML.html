💡 Idiot's guide to the company — What CML Microsystems actually does

CML makes the specialist chips that let machines talk to each other over the airwaves — the mixed-signal, radio-frequency and microwave semiconductors inside two-way radios, satellite links, industrial sensors and defence kit. It doesn't make phones or laptops; it sells the small, high-reliability parts that go into other companies' professional communications gear.

Where it's based

Head office is Oval Park in Maldon, Essex, with design, test and sales arms in the USA (a Silicon Valley operation, MwT, bought in 2023) and Asia. It outsources the actual chip fabrication and does its own testing.

How it earns

Two ways. Mostly it designs a chip then sells it by the reel, year after year — high-margin, repeatable product revenue. Increasingly it also gets paid up front to design custom silicon for a big customer (NRE — one-off design fees), which pays the bills now but at a thinner reward. A 12-year satnav deal signed in 2025 is currently in that design-fee phase.

Recent structural events

A multi-year reshaping: bought MwT (2023) to widen the microwave range, restructured UK research (2025, took a write-off), and sold surplus land at the Essex site for £7m to shore up cash. Now calls itself a pure-play communications semiconductor company chasing four markets: professional radio, network infrastructure, industrial IoT, and aerospace & defence.

The investment argument in one line: a debt-free, dividend-paying niche chip firm that has just been through a bruising demand slump and a self-funded rebuild — the bet is that the order book is turning and profit follows the revenue back up.
