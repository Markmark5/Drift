💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Kier Group actually does

What it is

Kier builds and looks after the UK's big public stuff — roads, railways, water works, prisons, hospitals and schools. Around 20,000 people, listed on the London market, FTSE 250.

Where it's based

Head office in Manchester; sites and depots across the whole UK. Nearly all revenue is British and most of it comes, directly or indirectly, from government bodies.

How it makes money

Two engines. Infrastructure (just over half of sales) designs, builds and maintains roads, rail and water networks under long framework deals. Construction (a bit under half) puts up buildings — prisons, hospitals, schools — mostly for the public sector. A small Property arm develops sites with its own money; from FY27 it stops taking on new schemes and hands the cash back to the balance sheet.

Who controls the price. Mostly the customer's framework terms — but about 95% of work is either cost-reimbursable or priced in two stages, so Kier rarely carries an old-fashioned fixed-price gamble on its own.

What's changed recently

A whole new top team inside a year (CEO Nov-25, CFO and Construction boss Jan-26), two divisions merged into one Infrastructure unit, the Property wind-down announced, and the balance sheet finally in average net cash — the first time in over a decade.

The bet, in one line: the UK has to spend on water, power, prisons and hospitals for years, and Kier is on the frameworks that hand that work out.
