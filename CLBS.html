💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Celebrus Technologies actually does

What it is

Celebrus makes software that watches every click, tap and swipe on a big company's website or app — live, as it happens — and turns that into a clean record of each customer, even before they log in. Banks and insurers use it to personalise offers and to spot fraudsters mid-session. It sells three-year software subscriptions, mostly hosted in its own "Celebrus Cloud". It used to be called D4t4 Solutions and once resold a lot of other firms' hardware; that sideline is nearly wound down.

Where it's based

Sunbury-on-Thames, UK; listed on London's AIM market; reports in US dollars because most customers are big American and global financial firms. Operates in 30+ countries.

How it makes money

Subscription licences and managed services for its own platform (the part it calls Celebrus ARR, $15.0m a year), plus some older non-Celebrus managed services ($5.3m), a shrinking slice of pass-through hardware, and interest on a $32.5m cash pile.

Who controls the price. Enterprise deals are negotiated one by one; big banks have muscle, but once installed the product is sticky — nobody left in FY26, though two banks shrank their contracts after selling parts of themselves.

What's changed recently

From April 2025 licence money is booked monthly over the contract instead of yearly up front, which slashes reported revenue for about three years without changing the cash. The sales team was rebuilt into three separate units (find leads, win new names, grow existing accounts) after a year of missed new-business targets.

The bet, in one line: a sticky, cash-rich niche platform — the question is whether it can win new customers fast enough to matter.
