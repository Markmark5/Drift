💡 Idiot's guide to the company — What LBG Media plc actually does
What they are

The LADbible people. A Manchester outfit that makes short, shareable stuff for young adults — videos, memes, sport clips, news — under brands like LADbible, SPORTbible, Tyla and (in the US) Betches. Half a billion people follow them across Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and their own websites. Listed on AIM, September year-end.

How the money comes in — two ways

Direct: big brands (Google, Tesco, Netflix, KFC) pay LBG to make ads that don't feel like ads — branded content dropped into feeds young people actually watch. LBG controls this one: its own sales team wins the work, signs the deal, sends the bill. Lower profit per pound, but it's theirs.

Indirect: the platforms pay them a cut. Facebook and friends sell adverts next to LBG's content and share the money; same for adverts on LBG's own websites. Easy money when it flows — but the tap is owned by Facebook and Google, not LBG, and lately they've been turning it down. Higher profit per pound, none of it under LBG's control.

The June 2026 twist

They bought Uncovered — a ~120-person London agency, the #1 TikTok partner in Europe — for £26.8m initial, part-paid with their first ever bank borrowing (£35m HSBC facility). It pushes even more of the business into Direct, on purpose.

One-line version: a youth-media machine swapping easy platform money it can't control for harder-won brand money it can — and the whole share-price argument is whether the swap finishes before the old money runs out.

Turn methodology & restatement basis

Comparison basis. Flow metrics score YEAR-ON-YEAR (H1-vs-H1, H2-vs-H2); the banded Δrate (±2.0pp) sets the emoji — computed in one script, cells transcribe. pp values live here, never in cells. Margin is a rate metric: pp move vs same period last year, ±2.0pp band (Mar-26 −12.4pp 🔴; Mar-25 +1.4pp 🟡; Sep-25† −3.1pp 🔴).

Year-end change (Dec→Sep, 2024). FY24 statutory = 9 months (Jan–Sep-24), a stub never scored. The company's printed proforma 12m-to-Sep-24 (86.2 / D 43.9 / I 41.2ʳ / E 24.5) supplies the Sep-grain year: H1ᵖ Mar-24 = printed comparative (Jun-25 RNS: 17.9/20.8/38.8/10.3, margin 26.4%); H2†ᵖ Sep-24 = PF12m − H1 Mar-24. H1ᵒ Jun-24 = old-grain interim printed during transition; overlaps Mar-24 by Q1-24, so the two never compare to each other — each scores only its own matched prior (Jun-24 vs Jun-23; Mar-24 = series-first, no score).

Derivations & tie-outs. H2† = FY − H1: Dec-22† (17.2/20.0/38.0/14.1), Dec-23† (17.9/21.8/40.3/14.4), Sep-24†ᵖ (26.0/20.4/47.4/14.2), Sep-25† (30.4/17.0/48.3/13.0). Segment tie-out D+I vs Total leaves an "Other" residual of 0.6–1.0 per year — consistent with the printed Other line (~£1m). No second derivation route exists (no Q prints); † turns are watch-flags until print-corroborated. H2 Sep-25† Indirect −16.7% was the earliest matched-basis turn in the record — corroborated in print by Mar-26's −40.8%.

Δrate anchors & flags. Each Δrate compares the cell's YoY to the last available like-grain YoY: on the new Sep grain, Mar-25 and Sep-25† are first-YoY cells — their like-grain priors (Mar-24, Sep-24†ᵖ) carry no YoY, so no Δrate exists and both take no score. Anchoring them to old Dec-grain rates (Jun-24ᵐ, Dec-23†) was a cross-basis defect corrected at audit: different 6-month windows, and Jun-24's rate is ᵐ-inflated by Betches. Mar-26 is the first cell with a valid same-grain Δrate (−40.8% vs Mar-25 +17.8% = −58.6pp 🔴↘). Rank 5 (margin) is a RATE metric: it scores the pp move year-on-year directly — a first derivative, not a Δrate — so it is unaffected by the grain change and keeps valid scores at Mar-25 (+1.4pp 🟡) and Sep-25† (−3.1pp 🔴). That makes the margin row the only rank carrying a scored warning before the Buy/Sell RNS. The YoY levels themselves remain valid throughout — Sep-25† Indirect −16.7% is a genuine first matched-basis decline and enters the turn inventory; it simply carries no cell score. Zero-crossing guard checked on the rendered +240%: base £3.0m exceeds the £2.5m absolute band (10% of series max), percentage stands. ʳ: FY24 PF Indirect 40.7 (Jan-25, unaudited) → 41.2 (Feb-26 audited comparative, governs). Uncovered payback — fast, and interest already netted

  • Zeus: net EBITDA contribution in FY27E (first full year) ≈ £3.5m, = a 2–3pp group-margin improvement — and that is after the ≈£1.1m/yr RCF interest. So the debt-service cost of the deal is already inside the accretion figure.

🍺 IDIOT'S GUIDE TO THE EBITDA BRIDGE — why profit went down (£22m → £17.5m)
📱➡️🤝 The Instagram-to-agency swapn/qEvery pound that shifts from a platform paying LBG a slice of ad money to a brand paying an agency fee straight-up is a pound with a smaller cut taken out at the till. Direct's growing fast — the cut per pound is just thinner.
📉 The Indirect tap keeps drippingn/qThe higher-margin half of the business — the bit that just sat there earning a platform cut — keeps shrinking, and there's no fix on the shelf for a Facebook algorithm.
🧑‍💼 New hires, no wins yetn/qDirect sales and ops people started this half; their pay cheques land now, the deals they're meant to close land later.
So in one sentence: Direct is winning more work, but each win pays a bit less to the bottom line, and the new hires needed to win it haven't started earning their keep yet. Profit goes back up when: Indirect stops shrinking so there's less lost margin to backfill, or the new Direct hires start landing enough wins to cover their own wages. One thing to keep half an eye on: about £1 in every £4 of that booming Direct work comes from Google alone — handy while it lasts, but a lot riding on one name. Rows n/q — the RNS names the mix shift and the investment as the two drivers of the £4.5m guide cut but doesn't split it into priced amounts.
