💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Genus plc actually does

What it is

Genus sells the genetics that farm animals are bred from. Two businesses: PIC (pigs) and ABS (dairy and beef cattle). It owns elite breeding animals and sells their semen, embryos and breeding stock to farmers worldwide, so the farmers' next generation of animals grows faster and converts feed to meat or milk more efficiently.

Where the money comes from

The best bit is royalties — farmers pay a recurring fee per animal produced from PIC genetics, rather than a one-off sale. That's stickier and higher-margin, which is why management talks about "royalty revenue" as the number that matters. ABS has been the weaker side and spent FY24–26 on a cost-and-efficiency turnaround called the Value Acceleration Programme (VAP).

Where it's based

Headquartered in Basingstoke, UK; research labs in Madison, Wisconsin; sells into 85+ countries. Financial year ends 30 June.

The big recent event

In January 2026 Genus sold control of its Chinese pig business (PIC China) into a joint venture with a state-backed local partner, BCA — keeping a 49% stake and pocketing roughly £111m of cash. From FY26 onwards PIC China no longer sits inside the group's own sales line; it's an equity stake instead. It also finally won US and Canadian regulatory approval for its flagship PRRS-Resistant Pig gene edit — a potential future growth engine, not yet a revenue line.

The argument: a stickier royalty pig business plus a genuinely-recovering cattle business, now sitting on a near-debt-free balance sheet, with a gene-editing option that may or may not pay off.
