💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Kainos Group actually does

What it is

Kainos writes and runs computer systems for other organisations. Three separate businesses under one roof, and they earn money in quite different ways.

Where it's based

Head office in Belfast, listed in London, with people in 18 countries — 3,885 of them at the last count, up six hundred in six months.

How it makes money

Digital Services builds the government systems ordinary people actually touch — booking a driving test, border checks, NHS appointments. Paid for delivery, on contracts that now run four or five years, and defence work has grown big enough to get its own line. It increasingly leads consortia of other suppliers on the biggest contracts, which wins more and keeps less of each pound.

Workday Services installs somebody else's software. Workday sells big companies their HR and finance system; Kainos is one of the most experienced firms in the world at plugging it in and keeping it running.

Workday Products is the jewel. Six of its own software tools that sit alongside Workday and check it is working properly. Customers pay every year to keep using them: over 750 customers, and yearly subscription income has gone from £28m to £96m in four years.

Who controls the price. Mostly Kainos, with two catches. Government buyers run competitive tenders and can simply pause spending — that happened after the 2024 election and cost a whole year of growth. And the products are priced per user, which the company itself flagged as a thing to watch if artificial intelligence lets customers employ fewer people.

What's changed recently

A bad FY25, a sharp FY26 recovery (revenue +17%), then an FY27 that has started faster still. A small Canadian acquisition, Davis Pier, in September 2025. A new AI business, Actonomy, launched July 2026. And from November 2026 the company counts its order book by full contract value rather than orders received — both counts are still published.

The bet, in one line: you are buying the UK Government's favourite digital supplier mid-boom, with a fast-growing software annuity attached, and betting the expensive temporary staff go home before the government spending cycle does.

Operator override is part of the design.
