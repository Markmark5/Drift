💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Kainos Group actually does

What it is

Kainos writes and runs computer systems for other organisations. Three separate businesses under one roof, and they earn money in quite different ways.

Where it's based

Head office in Belfast, listed in London, with people in 18 countries. Just over 3,475 of them.

How it makes money

Digital Services builds the government websites and systems ordinary people actually touch — booking a driving test, renewing a passport, NHS appointments. Paid by the day, per person on the job, on contracts that now run four or five years.

Workday Services installs somebody else's software. Workday sells big companies their HR and finance system; Kainos is one of the most experienced firms in the world at plugging it in and keeping it running.

Workday Products is the jewel. Four of its own software tools that sit alongside Workday and check it's working properly. Customers pay every year to keep using them, which is why this slice is the one to watch: over 600 customers now, and yearly subscription income has gone from £28m to £89m in four years.

Who controls the price. Mostly Kainos, but with two catches. Government buyers run competitive tenders and can simply pause spending — that happened after the 2024 election and cost a whole year of growth. And the products are priced per user, which the company itself flagged in May 2026 as a problem worth watching if artificial intelligence lets customers employ fewer people.

What's changed recently

A bad FY25 — revenue fell for the first time since 2009 — then a sharp recovery in FY26, revenue up 17% to £431m. A small Canadian acquisition, Davis Pier, in September 2025. Profit has not kept pace with sales, because Kainos has been hiring expensive contractors to deliver the work.

The bet, in one line: you are buying a recovering government supplier that happens to own a fast-growing software annuity, and betting the expensive temporary staff go home before the sales growth does.
