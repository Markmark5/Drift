💡 Idiot's guide to the company — What Staffline Group plc actually does

What it is

Staffline is the UK's biggest supplier of blue-collar temps — the people who stack supermarket shelves, load lorries and pack warehouses. When a food or logistics firm needs 500 extra pairs of hands for the week before Christmas, Staffline finds them, employs them, pays them, and bills the client for the hours worked. It's headquartered in Nottingham and listed on London's junior market (AIM).

How it makes money

Two engines. The big one — roughly nine pounds in every ten of profit — is GB temp staffing: it charges the client a small margin on top of each worker's wage, so profit rises and falls with the sheer number of hours its temps clock. The small one is Ireland, where alongside temps it also places people into permanent jobs and pockets a one-off fee each time — a fee that dries up fast when employers get nervous, which makes it an early warning light for confidence.

Who holds the purse strings

A handful of giant customers. A single new logistics contract (with the Culina group, signed May-25) was big enough to move the whole company's numbers, which cuts both ways — one client leaving would hurt just as much.

Recent shape

Sold off its troubled PeoplePlus welfare-to-work arm, so today's business is a cleaner, thinner staffing pure-play. Profit has climbed three years running and the company has been buying back its own shares — the share count has shrunk about a quarter since 2023.

The argument in one line: a cheap, cash-generative shelf-stacking machine grinding out steady profit upgrades that the share price has so far ignored — with the catch that all the eggs sit in one low-margin, big-customer basket.

🍺 IDIOT'S GUIDE TO THE PROFIT BEFORE TAX BRIDGE — why profit went up (£0.6m → £2.9m)
📦 A heatwave, a World Cup, and 2.3 million extra hours+£0.8mGB temps worked 23.8 million hours, up 10.7% — sunshine sold drinks, football sold crisps, and someone had to pack it all. By June the hours were running 16.1% up. Extra profit from the British side: £0.8m.
👮 Dublin's finest, on Staffline's books+£0.7mIreland's profit doubled from £0.7m to £1.4m — a third of the jump in one small division. The engine: 33.3% more fees for filling permanent jobs, with a contract recruiting for the Irish police doing much of the hiring.
🤝 No goodbye-money this year+£0.4mLast year's first half carried £0.4m of redundancy payouts from the slim-down. This year: none. That saving lands straight in the profit line without anyone lifting a finger.
🏦 The bank's bill got smaller+£0.4mInterest paid fell from £2.7m to £2.3m — the Bank of England cut rates from 4.75% to 3.75%, and Staffline chased customer payments in quicker, so less was borrowed against unpaid invoices for less of the time.
So in one sentence: a sun-and-football summer of extra hours, a doubling Irish desk, no redundancy cheques to write and a smaller interest bill turned £0.6m into £2.9m. Profit falls when: hours from September onwards get measured against months that already had the big logistics contract in them, the Irish police stop hiring, or rising unpaid customer invoices (£162m, up a third) force more borrowing and the bank's bill grows again.
