💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Severfield plc actually does

The one-liner

Severfield is Britain's biggest steel-frame builder. When someone puts up a warehouse, a stadium, a data centre or a nuclear plant, Severfield designs, cuts and bolts together the giant steel skeleton that holds the whole thing up. It's the bones of the building, not the bricks.

Where it's based & how it's built

Head office is in Dalton, North Yorkshire. It runs six fabrication factories across the UK and Europe — roughly 150,000 tonnes of steel a year, about 1,800 staff. Steel comes in as raw sections, gets engineered and painted in the sheds, then ships to site to be craned into place.

How it makes money

Two engines. The big one is UK & Europe construction — one-off contracts won by competitive tender, so the profit on each job depends entirely on how tight the bidding was when they signed it. When the market's quiet, they bid cheap to keep the factories busy, and margins get crushed — which is exactly what happened in FY25–FY26. The smaller, faster-growing engine is India (JSSL), a joint venture with steel giant JSW: Severfield owns a slice and books its share of the profit. India's the growth story.

The recent shake-up

New CEO (Paul McNerney, arrived early FY26) and new CFO ran a strategic review, closed the loss-making Modular Solutions arm, took a pile of one-off charges, and pivoted the whole company from "chase volume" to "chase margin." They also suspended the dividend to protect the balance sheet, promising to bring it back once cash allows.

The argument in one line: a cyclical steel-frame builder caught at the bottom of its own margin cycle, with order books already refilling and a self-help margin programme — the bet is that the trough is passing.

🍺 IDIOT'S GUIDE TO THE PROFIT BRIDGE — why profit went up (£10.5m → ~£13.5m)
🧱 The cheap-job hangover clears+n/qFor two years Severfield took dirt-cheap steel jobs just to keep the factory sheds busy and the welders paid — work that barely made a penny once the bills were in. Those jobs finally get finished and shipped out the door in FY27, so they stop bleeding into the numbers and dragging profit down.
🏗️ Data-centre gold rush lands+n/qThe internet needs somewhere to live, and that means giant steel-framed server sheds going up across the UK, Germany and Sweden. Severfield's been winning a stack of these — better-paid, higher-quality work than the cheap stuff — and it starts feeding through the factories and into the profit line this year.
⏳ The good stuff arrives fashionably late£0 → FY28Here's the catch: the really juicy, fat-margin contracts don't actually start getting built until late in the year. So FY27 only gets a nibble of the good dinner — the full plate lands in FY28. Management have been upfront that this year is a stepping stone, not the destination.
🇮🇳 India keeps quietly winning+n/qWhile everyone watches the UK, the Indian joint venture just keeps bagging orders — data-centre work plus repeat business from its steel-giant partner JSW building out its own sites. Its order book's at a record, so a bigger and bigger slice of India's profit drops into the Group pot each year.
So in one sentence: profit inches up because the loss-leader work rolls off and better-paid jobs take its place — but the real money's been deliberately parked in FY28. Profit goes back up harder when: those late high-margin contracts actually start being built (H2 FY27 into FY28) and the fatter order book finally reaches the bottom line.
