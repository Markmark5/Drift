💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Currys plc actually does

What it is

Currys sells televisions, laptops, washing machines, fridges and phones — and then sells you the credit to pay for them, the person who installs them, the plan that fixes them when they break, and the mobile contract that goes in the phone. It is the last big electricals chain standing in Britain, and the biggest one in Scandinavia. 691 shops, six countries, about 24,000 staff.

Where it's based

Head office in London. Two businesses that barely touch each other: UK & Ireland, trading as Currys, 296 shops, 59% of sales; and the Nordics, trading as Elkjøp and Elgiganten, 395 shops across Norway, Sweden, Denmark and Finland, 41% of sales. The financial year ends on the Saturday nearest the end of April.

How it makes money

Selling a fridge earns Currys very little — a couple of pence in every pound reaches profit. The money is in what comes attached to it. Credit: £1.2bn of sales were bought on Currys' own finance, and those customers come back and spend twice as much over their lifetime. Repairs and protection: 11.6m plans on the books and 1,500 engineers. And iD Mobile, its own phone network, now 2.6m people paying every month. Currys says a sale with everything attached earns eight times what the product alone earns.

Who controls the price. Not Currys. Samsung, LG, Apple and Sony set what a television costs, and Amazon and AO set what anyone can charge for it. What Currys controls is how many customers walk out with the installation, the insurance and the SIM card — and how much it spends on staff, shops and lorries. Both engines were pushed hard this year.

What's changed recently

Greece was sold in April 2024 for £156m. Elliott and JD.com both circled the business in February 2024 and both walked away. The pension hole that dominated the accounts for a decade is now £6m and effectively closed. Alex Baldock, chief executive for eight years, leaves on 3 August 2026; Fredrik Tønnesen, who runs the Nordics, replaces him.

The bet, in one line: that Scandinavia keeps recovering and Britain stops standing still — because right now one of the two engines is doing all the work.

Turn methodology & restatement basis

Comparison basis per rank. R1 and R5 are rate metrics: the score is the percentage-point move year-on-year, taken directly, banded ±3.0pp. R2 and R4 are flows: the year-on-year figure is displayed, and the score is the banded change in that year-on-year figure versus the same half a year earlier, ±2.0pp. R3 is a snapshot: sequential change in cells, year-on-year on the aggregate row only.

Display basis. Half-grain flow cells show year-on-year; the sequential comparison is computed and retained here, never displayed. For the Buy/Sell half, sequential reads +£27m (R2) and +£120m (R4) — both are Christmas seasonality, not signal, which is why this grain scores year-on-year.

Fallback rule, stated because it is used. Where the prior-year year-on-year figure does not exist, the score falls back to the year-on-year figure itself against the band. This applies to four cells: R2 and R4 at H1 FY23 and H2 FY23.

Why the like-for-like band is ±3.0pp and not the ±2.0pp default. The company prints like-for-like rounded to whole percentage points. Differencing two figures each rounded to ±0.5pp leaves ±1.0pp of uncertainty in the move; a ±2.0pp band would sit close enough to that noise floor to manufacture scores. Nordic amplitude in this record, (12)% to +8%, comfortably supports the wider band. This was handled at Stage 2d rather than by marking ten cells low-precision.

Zero-crossing guard, fired twice, both on Rank 2. Off the £3m H1 FY23 base, H1 FY24 rendered +300% — above the +200% check — so it is scored on absolute change, +£9m against the £14m absolute band. H1 FY25's score would have been computed against that voided +300%, producing a spurious −250pp red, so it too is scored absolutely at +£6m. Both carry ‡. Elkjøp's profit tripling off a near-zero base is arithmetically enormous and analytically small.

Absolute bands: R2 £14m · R3 0.26m · R4 £17m. R1 and R5 are rates and have none.

Units, one per rank, stated once: R1 % · R2 £m · R3 millions of subscribers · R4 £m · R5 %.
