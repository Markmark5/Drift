💡Idiot's guide to the company — What discoverIE Group plc actually does

What it is

discoverIE designs and makes bespoke electronic bits — sensors, connectors, magnetics, power supplies — that get buried inside other companies' industrial kit. It doesn't sell to you or me; it sells the fiddly custom components an equipment-maker can't easily buy off a shelf, which is why once its part is designed into a customer's product, it tends to stay there for that product's whole life.

Where it's based

UK-listed (FTSE 250), run from Guildford, but the real business is spread across mainland Europe, the UK, China, Sri Lanka, India and North America — around 4,600 staff in 21 countries.

How the money works

Two halves. Magnetics & Controls and Sensing & Connectivity — both making custom components aimed at markets set to grow for years whatever the economy does: industrial automation, medical, renewable energy, transport electrification, security. Growth comes two ways: winning new "design-ins" that lock a part into a customer's next product (organic), and buying up small specialist component makers to bolt on (acquisitions — 30 deals in 15 years). The order book and design-win pipeline are the tell for where organic sales go next.

What changed lately

A three-deal acquisition burst in FY27 — Storm (Dec-25), Trival (Apr-26) and 3Gmetalworx ($67.5m, announced May-26, awaiting regulatory clearance) — roughly £95m committed in six months, the largest such run in the Group's history, pushing borrowing up to about 2.2x a year’s profit before the planned pay-down to 1.8x.

The investment case in one line: a serial compounder that buys niche component specialists, embeds them in customers' products for the long haul, and funds the next deal from reliable cash — worth owning while design wins and the order book keep climbing and the debt keeps falling.

Turn methodology & restatement basis
Turn methodology. Rank 1 (Order book) is a snapshot metric — value compared position-to-position at its own disclosure grain (H1-vs-H1; FY-vs-FY); cross-type comparisons (H→FY) are not scored. Ranks 2 and 3 (organic orders and revenue %) are flow metrics — compared like-period-to-like-period only (Q1-vs-Q1, H1-vs-H1; never H1-vs-9M). Sequential QonQ is the primary trigger for these ranks: any negative sequential fires 🔴↘ regardless of year-comp; year-comp rate delta (±5pp Q-band) is secondary. Rank 4 (margin) is a period rate — H1-vs-H1 and FY-vs-FY only, band ±2.0pp; direction governs when inside band. Rank 5 (design wins) is a snapshot — direction vs prior same-type print. HARD GATE: no half or year is scored until its derivable quarters are computed with QonQ.

🍺IDIOT'S GUIDE TO THE ADJUSTED PBT BRIDGE — why profit went up in FY26 (£49.9m → £51.9m, +4%)
🏭 Selling more, the hard way
+£0.5m op. profit
Sales grew 5% to £443m, but day-to-day profit only crept up 1% to £61.0m — because they deliberately spent ahead, kitting out factories in Asia and hiring sales and engineering muscle in the US and Europe. Jam tomorrow: the investment lands now, the payback comes later.
🔧 Building for a bigger tomorrow
held it to +1%
That same spending is why the profit slice of each pound of sales dipped a touch (13.8%, down 0.4 of a point). It's a choice, not a stumble — they're buying capacity now to hit a 17%-margin goal by 2030, part-funded by squeezing costs elsewhere.
💸 The bank takes a smaller cut
+3pts of PBT
Here's the lift: less money owed and falling interest rates meant a smaller bill for borrowed cash. That's what lifts whole-company profit up 4% when the shop-floor profit only rose 1% — the gap is pure interest saving. Net debt fell £14m to £80.5m over the year.
🪙 More left over per share
EPS +4% → 40.3p
After a lighter interest and tax bill, each share earned 40.3p, up from 38.7p — the tenth straight year of earnings growth (14% a year averaged over the decade). Enough to nudge the dividend up 4% to 13.0p and still cover it three times over.
So in one sentence: they sold more and paid the bank less, so profit rose 4% even though they poured money into future growth that flattened this year's margin. Profit accelerates from here when: the Asia and US investment starts earning its keep, net debt keeps falling faster than rates, and the three new acquisitions (bought for £95m) bed in at their higher margins.

Workflow specification — Longitudinal Drift Tracker v3.11 (reference)

The full framework this scorecard is built to. Reference only — it renders nothing and scores nothing.

LONGITUDINAL DRIFT TRACKER — v3.11

Purpose: detect direction-of-travel changes across a company's full RNS history before they show in reported numbers. Multi-RNS turn detector, not a single-announcement scorer.

Operating model: one project per ticker. Analysis runs IN CHAT in a single gated turn; the HTML is built only at GO, from chat-approved content, with no re-derivation. Thereafter the operator pastes each new RNS for an incremental read. A datablock, if supplied, is a consensus/C-series yardstick only — it never seeds a table.

  • Soft signals lead, hard signals confirm, and the lag between them is the edge.
  • The ticker's key forward metric leads even the soft signals — and its disclosure breaks before its level does.
  • The aggregate can hide the turn. A record half can be built on a fading quarter — the latest sub-period is scored separately, and before its parent.
  • Sequential QonQ is the earliest hard-number turn signal in printed figures. YoY is 12–18 months late and is not used for scoring.
  • A company's own guide is the earliest number of all: a guide moved is a turn event in its own right.

The most recent RNS is the deliverable's centre of gravity: it is the Buy/Sell RNS, and the trailing 12 months must be exact.

ARCHITECTURE
Chat (turn 1): Manifest · Ledger · per-metric forward tables · §2.0 read · Text+G · Drift · B/S sweep · draft verdict — all correctable before commit.
HTML (turn 2): Verdict card · §1 Ledger · §2 unified forward grid · §2.0 · §3 Text+G · §4 Drift matrix. Forward signals only; no backbone tables, no statutory columns.
Statutory figures read directly from each H1/FY RNS where a wedge check needs them — never rendered as tables, never scored.

TURN 1 — THE READ (fixed output order, then STOP)
FY-label anchor: all period labels derive from the company's own stated periods in the RNS, never from filenames or price data. A change in stated year-end is itself a Drift event (surfaced as the FY-end pill).
Reading standard: every qualifying RNS — all narrative sections read in full; statutory tables/notes swept by targeted search. Grep alone is never a read. Confirm as a per-document METHOD LIST (FULL / NARR+SWEEP).
Two-tier accuracy: older than 12 months = transcription grade; trailing 12 months = every figure verified directly, every derivable quarter computed, exact.

1. Document Manifest — every file: date · label · qualifying vs noise (by CONTENT not title). Sets grid width: Q1/Q3 TUs present → 17-cell grid; absent → 9-cell.
2. §1 Metric Ledger — 4–5 scored metrics ranked by lead time. Rank 1 is the ticker's tell, cited with evidence. On quarterly disclosers, quarterly revenue qualifies. EPS never scored unless it demonstrably leads; statutory never scored. Bands: H-grain ±2.0pp · Q-grain ±5.0pp.
3. §2 Per-metric forward tables — one block per pick, from first print, no truncation. Grid blocks = ledger rows. Rows: Value · Δ vs prior · Turn, plus period line. Withheld-against-cadence = ❌ (feeds Drift). Quarterly derivations Q2†=H1−Q1, Q4†=FY−9M, cross-verified. Turn arithmetic computed in ONE script, comparison basis hard-coded per metric type. Sequential trigger (Q-grain): any negative QonQ fires 🔴↘. Rate trigger (banded). Leading metrics scored on direction, never level. HARD GATE: no half/year scored until its derivable quarters are computed.
4. §2.0 Most-recent-RNS current-state read — four checks: Sequential · Same-type-vs-last-year · Every derivable sub-period · Guidance-vs-prior-guide. Output: four reads (collapsed drawer) + Driver Bridge + single current-state bar (bridge left, bar right, 50/50).
   Driver Bridge: decomposes the guided profit line for the Buy/Sell period. Verbatim header: "🍺 IDIOT'S GUIDE TO THE [LINE] BRIDGE — why profit went [up/down] (£Xm → £Ym)". Max 4 rows: driver · signed contribution · tint · clause. Contributions only from figures printed in the RNS or grid; unquantified = n/q. Language: "idiot's guide" register — everyday mechanism words (banned: comp, constant currency, structural, mix-shift, basis, gearing, operating line, drag, headwind, tailwind, leverage, run-rate, normalised, underlying), exact numbers, one wry aside, one vivid image, invented fresh from THIS company's world. Caption: "So in one sentence:" + reversal conditions. Bridge explains fired conditions; never fires one, seeds nothing, counts toward nothing.
   Current-state bar: ≤100 words carrying the resulting colour (🟢/🟡/🔴 TURNING UP / STABLE—WATCH / TURNING DOWN) + any load flag.
5. §3 Text + G — merged table: Date · Doc · G · Guide met? · Verbatim phrase · Score. Every print (min 3yr) gets a row. G column fires vs the company's own prior guide (🔴 CUT / 🟢 RAISE / flat / —). "Guide met?" resolves only at a results row, one trail line per guide vs the final actual (conservatism/rashness read). Score per lexicon, profit line, verbatim quote mandatory.
6. §4 Drift — Disclosure Matrix. Every qualifying doc a column; manifest count = column count. Cells: ✅ quantified · 🟡 qualitative · ❌ verified blank vs cadence · ⊘ expected update not issued. Every cell document-verified. First note reserved for the rightmost column.
7. Silent balance-sheet sweep (every results doc; renders on a hit). Tier 1 (⛔): net cash→net debt crossover; net debt rising 2+ periods without disclosed acquisition; going-concern escalation; covenant stress; ND/EBITDA ~2.5–3.0x. Tier 2 (note; 2+ = flag). Solvency grep. Wedge check adjusted vs statutory.
8. Draft verdict — direction call · headline ≤25 words (a latest-RNS G fire leads) · meta row · Current State / Fired / Caveat / Next ≤18 words · bullets.

PAUSE — reply AUDIT (Stages A–E) or GO.

INFLECTION TRIGGER — 2 OF 3. Alert fires on 2+ conditions converging same direction. B/S outside the count; Tier-1 = automatic ⛔.
Deterioration: 1. Hard/G — sequential QonQ decline; or 2+ consecutive 🔴; or half-vs-quarter divergence; or growth decelerating 2+; or margin compression; or a G CUT. 2. Text — falling 2+ prints or any ≤−1. 3. Drift — a leading metric withheld/qualitative vs cadence.
Green-shoot mirror: sequential growth resuming / 2+ 🟢↗ / margin expanding / G RAISE · lexicon rising 2+ from 0 or below · dropped metric re-quantified.
Single-condition fire = verdict note — except a G fire on the latest RNS, which always reaches the verdict.

LEXICON (−3 to +3):
🟢🟢🟢 "materially/substantially ahead"; guidance raised twice in window.
🟢🟢 "comfortably/well ahead", "ahead of expectations", "not less than £X", "ahead of management's expectations".
🟢 "slightly/marginally ahead", "upper end of the range", "confidence we will continue to grow".
⚪ "in line with market expectations", "on track", flat reiteration.
🔴 "broadly in line"; "Board's/management's expectations" replacing "market"; first "second-half weighted"; an update omitting an expectations anchor where prior ones carried one.
🔴🔴 "slightly below/short of"; a named unresolved headwind in the growth-carrying line; previously-numeric guidance gone qualitative; in-line made conditional; an explicit pre-flagged down-year guide.
🔴🔴🔴 "below expectations", "challenging conditions", explicit unflagged guidance cut.

NOTES RULE: max ONE bullet per table, ONE sentence — except §2 (one sub-entry per rank), §2.0 and verdict (up to THREE on the Buy/Sell RNS). A note survives only if forward-looking AND concerns the trailing 12 months.

DATA INTEGRITY: RNS governs external sources; later audited figure governs earlier "c." estimate. Never invent/estimate/annualise/fill. Missing = —, never 0. Derived figures marked †, workings shown once, second-route cross-verification. Guides never converted to actuals. Broker/datablock = yardstick only.
