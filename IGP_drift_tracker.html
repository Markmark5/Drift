💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Intercede actually does

What it is. Intercede sells software that hands out and manages digital ID credentials — the certificate on the smart card or phone that proves you are who you say you are before a door, a network or a file opens. The core product is MyID CMS.

Where it is. Head office in Lutterworth, Leicestershire, with a US office in Reston, Virginia. About 111 staff. Roughly 82% of sales are American.

How the money arrives. Three streams. Licences — a big one-off fee when a customer first buys, or expands, the right to use the software; lumpy, high margin, and entirely in the customer's gift as to timing. Professional services — day-rate work installing it; follows the licences a few months behind. Support and maintenance — an annual fee to keep it patched and supported, which renews on its own and now covers about two-thirds of sales. The first stream decides whether a year is good; the third decides whether a bad year still pays the bills.

Who controls the timing. Mostly US federal agencies and defence contractors. They rarely cancel, but they can sit on a purchase order for months, which is precisely what happened in the year to March 2026.

Recent event. Bought Authlogics in October 2022 for £2m to add password and multi-factor products. Nothing since, though £20m of cash sits waiting and management says it looked at 25 targets last year.

The argument: a small, debt-free company selling something governments are obliged to buy, currently priced for the delay rather than the demand — provided the delayed orders actually turn up.
