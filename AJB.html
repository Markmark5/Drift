💡Idiot's guide to the company — What AJ Bell plc actually does

What it is

One of the UK's biggest investment platforms — think of it as the shop and the shelves where people keep their pensions, ISAs and share dealing accounts. You put your money in, AJ Bell holds it, lets you buy funds and shares, and takes a small slice for running the place.

Where it's based

Manchester, with offices in London and Bristol. Listed on the London Stock Exchange, in the FTSE 250. Founded 1995 by Andy Bell; run today by CEO Michael Summersgill (took over Oct-2022) and CFO Peter Birch.

How it makes money

Two tolls on the money it holds. The big one is a small annual percentage on everyone's assets (the "ad valorem" charge) — more assets, more fee, so record inflows matter enormously. On top, a cut every time customers trade, plus a chunky slug of interest earned on the cash sitting in accounts. Two front doors: the Advised side (financial advisers using it for clients) and the bigger, faster-growing D2C side (ordinary people via the AJ Bell app and Dodl). A separate arm, AJ Bell Investments, runs its own cheap funds and model portfolios (MPS) and earns a management fee on those.

Recent structural events

Sold its Platinum SIPP/SSAS business (completed Nov-2025, £21.4m disposal gain). Cutting its core MPS management charge 0.15%→0.12% from 1-Oct-2026 to stay the cheapest option.

The argument in one line: a low-cost, scale-driven platform compounding customers and assets faster than it adds cost — where the fee it charges on a swelling asset base is the whole engine.

🍺IDIOT'S GUIDE TO THE PROFIT BRIDGE — why profit went up (£68.6m → £79.0m)
💷 A fatter pot to skim +£29.8m Sales rose £153.2m → £183.0m. Most of it is the little annual percentage AJ Bell takes on everyone's savings — the pot swelled to £108.7bn, so the same tiny slice cuts a much bigger cake. The slice itself even got a touch thicker (33.4p per £1,000 held, up from 32.4p).
🎰 Jumpy markets, busy tills n/q Tariff chaos and Budget nerves had customers buying and selling far more than usual — and AJ Bell clips a small fee off every single trade. Folded inside the £29.8m above; the company named it as a driver but didn't print its own number.
📣 Shouting costs money n/q The advertising and brand blitz that pulled in a record 79,000 new customers — this bill is guided to grow 28–30% across the full year, the fastest-rising cost in the shop, and they've said it'll run even harder in the second half because it's working.
🔧 Feeding the machine n/q Building the app and running the back office — tech spend guided up 20–22% for the year, everyday running costs up a calmer 7–9%. All the cost lines together ate £19.4m of the sales gain, which is why profit grew 15% while sales grew 19%.
So in one sentence: the savings pot they clip a percentage from got much bigger and busier, earning £29.8m more — then they poured nearly two-thirds of that into adverts and engineers, and still banked an extra £10.4m. Profit climbs faster when: the ad blitz eases off (they've told you it won't yet — each pound spent brings in customers worth more than a pound), and falls if jumpy markets calm down and the trading tills go quiet.
