💡Idiot's guide to the company — What AJ Bell plc actually does

What it is

One of the UK's biggest investment platforms — the shop and the shelves where people keep their pensions, ISAs and share-dealing accounts. You put your money in, AJ Bell holds it, lets you buy funds and shares, and takes a small slice for running the place.

Where it's based

Manchester, with offices in London and Bristol. Listed in London (FTSE 250). Founded 1995 by Andy Bell; run today by CEO Michael Summersgill (since Oct-2022) and CFO Peter Birch.

How it makes money

Two tolls on the money it holds. The big one is a small yearly percentage on everyone's savings — more savings held, more fee, so money flowing in matters enormously. On top: a fee every time customers trade, plus interest earned on cash sitting in accounts. Two front doors: the Advised side (financial advisers using it for clients) and the bigger, faster-growing D2C side (ordinary people via the AJ Bell app and Dodl). A separate arm, AJ Bell Investments, runs its own cheap funds and ready-made portfolios (MPS) and earns a management fee on those.

Who controls the price. AJ Bell sets its own charges; competition from other platforms is what keeps them low, and it has chosen to cut them as it grows.

What's changed recently

Sold its Platinum SIPP/SSAS business (completed Nov-2025, £21.4m gain on sale). Winding down its last outside SIPP administration deal, moving those customers onto its own D2C platform. Core MPS charge cut 0.15% → 0.12% from 1-Oct-2026.

The bet, in one line: a low-cost platform piling up customers and savings faster than it piles up costs — where the fee on an ever-bigger pot is the whole engine.

Stable facts, as at 21 May 2026; SIPP wind-down added 22 Oct 2026.
