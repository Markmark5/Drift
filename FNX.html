💡 Idiot's guide to the company — What Fonix plc actually does

The business in one line

Fonix is a London-based AIM-listed mobile payments and messaging intermediary, founded 2006. It sits between mobile network operators (EE, Vodafone, O2, and counterparts in Ireland and now Portugal) and enterprise clients — mainly broadcasters and media companies — enabling consumers to pay for things or enter competitions by tapping their phone bill.

How money flows

A viewer texts a premium-rate number to vote on a TV show or donate to charity. Fonix collects that payment from the mobile operator, takes its cut (the "gross profit"), and passes the rest to the client. The company's own financial metric is gross profit — not revenue — because its revenue line inflates with pass-through MNO charges that are irrelevant to performance.

Revenue streams

Mobile Payments (≈80% of GP): Carrier billing and SMS billing for competition entries, charity donations, and digital goods — primarily with broadcasters like ITV, Global (Heart, Capital, Radio X), Bauer, and RTÉ. Mobile Messaging (≈16% of GP): High-volume enterprise SMS delivery for corporate clients. Managed Services (≈4%): Campaign management fees, mostly charity telethons.

Products

Campaign Manager: The core platform — a hybrid CPaaS and payments product controlling broadcaster interactivity. PayFlex: Launched 2025, recovers failed mobile payments by routing via Apple Pay / Google Pay / PayPal. CompsPortal: Launched Dec-25, standalone competition entry portal with card payments. RCS/RichMessaging: Next-generation interactive messaging in trial.

International footprint

UK (core, c.86% of GP) · Ireland (established, growing, c.14% of GP) · Portugal (commercial launch Sep-25, early traction) · Switzerland (pilot Feb–Mar-26, second pilot Aug-26) · France (legal entity established, connectivity in progress) · Fifth market (connectivity established, launch targeted late FY27).

Key clients

ITV (contract renewed for 10th year in FY26), Global (renewed 3 years in FY26), Bauer, RTÉ, Channel 4, Channel 5 (CompsPortal), BBC Children in Need, Comic Relief.

Financial character

No debt. Highly cash-generative (EBITDA margins 74–80% of GP). Dividend policy: ≥75% of adjusted EPS. Share buybacks completed in FY26 (£2m). Adjusted PBT is the key profit metric; adjustments are minimal (share-based charges only, tiny relative to earnings).

Investment argument: a small, dominant intermediary in a defensible, recurring-revenue niche — scaling internationally with a replicable blueprint. As at 23 July 2026.
