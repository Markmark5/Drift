💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Genuit Group plc actually does

What it is

Genuit makes the pipes, drains, gutters, ducting and ventilation units that get buried in walls, under floors and beneath roads. Head office in Leeds, listed in London as GEN, around £600m of sales a year and roughly 3,300 staff. If the name Polypipe rings a bell, that is the same company under an older badge.

Where it's based

Almost entirely the United Kingdom, with small operations in Ireland and Italy. That matters more than it sounds: when British housebuilding stalls, there is no other geography to lean on.

How it makes money

Water is around seventy per cent of sales — plastic pipes and drainage sold to housebuilders, commercial developers and the water utilities. Climate is the rest — ventilation units, underfloor heating, and magnetic filters that keep central heating systems clean.

Who controls the price. Genuit does, but only up to a point and only slowly. Its biggest raw material is polymer, priced off oil, and when that jumps the company has to go to merchants and builders and ask for more. That took until May this year, which is why the first half looked so much worse than the last two months of it.

What's changed recently

Seven businesses bought since 2021, most recently Monodraught and Davidson in late 2025. Each one adds sales that were not there the year before, which is why the headline sales figure and the like-for-like one now tell very different stories. The reporting structure has been reshuffled twice, so older divisional figures do not line up with today's.

The bet, in one line: an unglamorous business with a real cost-discipline story, betting that self-help can outlast a British construction downturn now into its third year.

Notes — Ranks 1 to 5 Rank 1 — like-for-like revenue
  • H1 FY26 at −4.8% is the second consecutive negative print and fires the hard condition. That is the first positive like-for-like reading since the four months to April 2025.
  • Two-month stubs are not scored and never occupy a column — this one is a current-trading observation only.
Rank 2 — underlying operating profit
  • £43.9m against £44.6m, down 1.6%, versus +2.3% at the same point last year — a 3.9pp deceleration. Guidance for the full year was explicitly held, which is a reiteration of the May downgrade rather than a fresh cut.
  • The gap to statutory profit widened sharply: reported operating profit fell 30.1% to £26.2m on transformation and simplification charges. A widening wedge is a verdict bullet in its own right and is flagged in the sweep.
Rank 3 — underlying operating margin
  • Scores amber at −0.7pp because the reported figure sits inside the ±1.0pp band. The like-for-like margin fell 140bps to 13.7%. The divergence is the Davidson acquisition, which earned over 20% margin in the half and lifts the group average.

Rank 4 — new product vitality
  • Withheld at a half-year document for the first time in the record. The prior pattern was withheld at full year, retained at H1. It is now absent from both. Last printed value 15.3% at H1 FY25, against a target of 25% that itself stopped being stated after H1 FY24.
Rank 5 — cash conversion
  • 71.3% against 65.1%, up 620bps, and the only green cell on the Buy/Sell column. Management guides above 90% for the full year, consistent with the medium-term target. Genuine positive, though it partly reflects phasing rather than trading.
Turn methodology & restatement basis Comparison basis, hard-coded per metric type
  • Rate metrics (Ranks 1, 3, 4, 5) — the value is the rate. Scored on the change in that rate versus the seasonally matched prior period: H1 vs H1, H2 vs H2, FY vs FY. Never H1 vs H2. Line 2 carries the emoji and the pp movement.
  • Flow metrics (Rank 2) — value plus YoY% on line 1, comparing like period to like period. The scored element on line 2 is the change in that YoY growth rate versus the prior seasonally matched period — i.e. acceleration, not growth.
  • Snapshot metrics — none in this build. GEN publishes no order book, pipeline or backlog figure on a consistent basis, so no metric carries a sequential position-to-position comparison. Leverage was considered and dropped to the balance-sheet sweep, where the Tier-1 trigger already covers it.
  • Display basis — half-grain flow rows display seasonally matched YoY with the banded rate-delta as the scored element. Raw half-on-half movement is computed but never displayed in cells; it is seasonal at GEN (H2 margin is structurally above H1 in every year of the record) and would read as a turn when it is only the calendar.
  • Band — ±1.0pp at half grain, adjusted down from the framework default of ±2.0pp on sector amplitude. GEN's margin moves in a 20–140bps range; at ±2.0pp the margin rank could never fire, including the FY25 decline.
