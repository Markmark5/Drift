💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Beeks Financial Cloud actually does

What it is

Beeks rents out very fast computers to banks, brokers and stock exchanges — the kind that sit metres from an exchange's matching engine so a trade arrives a millionth of a second sooner.

Where it's based

Head office in Renfrew, near Glasgow; listed on AIM since 2017. Racks sit in trading data centres in London, New York, Chicago, Johannesburg, Sydney, Mexico City and elsewhere.

How it makes money

Private Cloud — a monthly rent for computers Beeks owns and runs. Steady, sticky, about two-thirds of sales.

Proximity Cloud — racks built for one big customer; a chunk of a five-year deal gets booked the moment the kit goes live, the rest drips in.

Exchange Cloud — the exchange resells Beeks racks to its own traders. Older deals paid a lump up front; newer ones pay Beeks a slice of what the traders spend.

Market Edge Intelligence — AI software that watches trading networks for trouble; licence and kit booked on delivery, support paid monthly.

Who controls the price. Big banks and exchanges negotiate hard and sign long contracts; once the kit is wired in, leaving is a pain, so renewals stick.

What's changed recently

Market Edge Intelligence won three customers in its first year (over $10m of contracts). A private network product and an AI partnership with OmniPhi launch in 2027, and a £5m Barclays credit line was agreed after June 2026.

The bet, in one line: every exchange that signs becomes a shop window selling Beeks racks to its own members.
