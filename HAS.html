💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Hays plc actually does

What it is

Hays is a recruitment firm. Companies pay it to find them staff — either permanent hires (Hays takes a one-off fee per placement) or temporary workers and contractors (Hays takes a slice of every hour worked). The money Hays keeps after paying the temps is called net fees, and that's the number that matters — turnover includes the temps' wages, which just pass through.

Where it's based

London-listed, operating in 23 countries. Germany is the biggest single market, then UK & Ireland and Australia. About two-thirds of net fees now come from temp and contracting work, one-third from permanent placements.

How it makes money

Two engines. Temp & Contracting hums along steadily — workers on assignment keep generating fees week after week. Perm is the boom-bust engine: fees only land when someone is actually hired, so when bosses get nervous, hiring freezes and Perm fees vanish fast.

Who controls the price. The job market does. Hays can cut its own costs but cannot make clients hire. Wage inflation helps fees; hiring freezes crush them.

What's changed recently

Three years of shrinking fees forced deep surgery: a third of fee-earners gone, 74 offices closed in FY26 alone, six European businesses sold, and a new "Momentum" plan promising £50m more cost savings in FY27. The restructuring bills ran £89.6m in FY26, so the accounts show a statutory loss even as underlying profit turned up.

The bet, in one line: costs have been cut faster than fees are falling, so any hiring recovery drops straight into profit — if Perm behaves.
