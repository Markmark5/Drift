💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Debenhams Group actually does

Who they are

Debenhams Group plc — the rebranded boohoo group — is a UK online retailer incorporated in Jersey, listed on AIM, and run out of Manchester. The Debenhams name was bought out of the old department store's administration years ago; the company later gave up its own boohoo name and put Debenhams above the door instead.

How the money actually works

They used to buy stock, sell it themselves, and keep the lot. Now most of what's sold on their sites belongs to other brands — those brands pay Debenhams a cut (a commission) for the shelf space and the checkout, and Debenhams books only that cut as its own revenue, not the full sale price. That's why total sales through the sites (GMV) can be miles ahead of what shows up as "revenue" in the numbers — the gap is stock the company never owned.

Who controls what

Four brand groups sit under one roof: Debenhams itself (now the biggest, bought back from administration), PrettyLittleThing and boohoo/Youth Brands (the original fast-fashion labels), and Karen Millen. Management in Manchester runs the shared warehouse, website plumbing, and back-office costs for all of them centrally.

What changed recently

The rebrand from boohoo group to Debenhams Group completed in March 2025. Alongside it, three warehouses were merged into one automated site in Sheffield, three separate tech systems were merged into one, and a swathe of supplier and lease contracts were renegotiated — the cost side of the business was rebuilt from the ground up at the same time as the name changed.

The one-line argument: a fast-fashion retailer that nearly drowned in its own stock has turned itself into a landlord for other people's stock instead — lower risk, better margins, if the traffic keeps coming.
