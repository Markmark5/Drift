💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Next 15 Group plc actually does

What it is

A collection of marketing, research and digital-consulting firms under one roof. Each firm keeps its own name — Transform, SMG, M Booth, Savanta, Pretzl and a dozen more — and Next 15 owns all or most of each.

Where it's based

Headquartered in London and listed on AIM. Roughly half its fees come from the US, so a strong pound shrinks its sales when they are turned back into sterling.

How it makes money

Clients pay fees for people's time: running a PR campaign, building a government website, measuring a brand, placing ads inside supermarket apps. Most work is project-by-project or on retainers that can be cancelled at short notice. The "net revenue" figure is what's left after paying outside suppliers — the money the firms keep for their own staff and profit.

Who controls the price. The client does. Big tech firms used to be the biggest spenders and have cut back hard since 2023. UK government departments now buy a growing share, mostly through Transform, and they buy through frameworks where Next 15 bids against rivals.

What's changed recently

In June 2025 it found possible serious misconduct at Mach49, a US venture-building firm it bought, stopped paying the sellers, and shut the business by January 2026. The sellers took it to arbitration; the hearing is over and a ruling is due this financial year. The company has also sold or part-sold several smaller firms (BYND, Blueshirt, BCA, Palladium, part of Elvis) and split the rest into "Track 1" firms it will grow and "Track 2" firms it may sell.

The bet, in one line: a shrinking group that has cut costs to hold its margin, betting a government-IT winner can make the whole thing grow again before the Mach49 bill arrives.
