💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Midwich Group plc actually does

What it is

Midwich is a middleman. It buys audio-visual kit — screens, speakers, projectors, lighting, the gear behind a boardroom video wall or a festival main stage — from the big manufacturers and sells it on to the installers and resellers who actually fit it. It doesn't make anything and it doesn't sell to you or me; it sits in the gap between the factory and the fitter, and takes a slice. About 24,000 trade customers, 50-odd countries, run from Diss in Norfolk.

How it makes money

Thin margins on big volume. Every £100 of kit that passes through leaves roughly £17.70 of gross profit on the counter — and nudging that number up by selling cleverer, more technical gear (live-events lighting, specialist audio) rather than plain commodity screens is the whole game. It grows two ways: winning share off rivals, and buying up smaller specialist distributors around the world.

Who controls the levers

Demand it can't control — corporate and education budgets, squeezed by high rates and cautious spending for two years. Margin and cost it can: pushing the technical mix, cutting overheads, managing debt against a 3.0x covenant ceiling. Stephen Fenby runs it; Adam Councell is the new finance chief (2025 handover).

Recent structural event

2025 was a reset year — a £27m write-off on a scrapped single-ERP software project turned a real £30.5m adjusted profit into a £30.5m statutory loss on paper, and the dividend was cut from 13.0p to 5.25p. The cash business kept working underneath the noise.

The argument: a share-gaining market leader in a cyclical trough, priced for the trough — the bet is that when corporate and education spending returns, the profit swings back hard.
