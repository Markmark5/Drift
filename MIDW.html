💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Midwich Group plc actually does

What it is

Midwich is a middleman. It buys audio-visual kit — screens, speakers, projectors, lighting, the gear behind a boardroom video wall or a festival main stage — from the big manufacturers, and sells it on to the installers and resellers who actually fit it. It doesn't make anything and it doesn't sell to you or me; it sits in the gap between the factory and the fitter, and takes a slice. About 24,000 trade customers, 50-odd countries, run from Diss in Norfolk.

How it makes money

Thin margins on big volume. Every £100 of kit that passes through leaves roughly £17.70 of gross profit on the counter — and nudging that number up by selling cleverer, more technical gear (live-events lighting, specialist audio) rather than plain commodity screens is the whole game. More technical mix = fatter margin. It grows two ways: winning share off rivals, and buying up smaller specialist distributors around the world.

Who controls the levers

Demand it can't control — corporate and education budgets, which have been squeezed by high rates and cautious spending for two years. Margin and cost it can: pushing the technical mix, cutting overheads, managing debt against a 3.0x covenant ceiling. Stephen Fenby runs it; Adam Councell is the new finance chief (2025 handover).

Recent structural event

2025 was a reset year — a £27m write-off on a scrapped single-ERP software project turned a real £30.5m adjusted profit into a £30.5m statutory loss on paper, and the dividend was cut from 13.0p to 5.25p. The cash business kept working underneath the noise.

The argument: a share-gaining market leader in a cyclical trough, priced for the trough — the bet is that when corporate and education spending returns, the operational gearing swings profit back hard.

Turn methodology & restatement basis

Comparison bases (hard-coded per metric type): snapshot metrics (order books, leverage) compare position-to-position at their own disclosure grain; flow metrics (Adj PBT) compare like-period-to-like-period only (H1-vs-H1, never H1-vs-9M); rate metrics (organic revenue, gross margin) are seasonally matched (H1-vs-H1, FY-vs-FY). No sequential QonQ trigger — MIDW is a half-year reporter, so the half is the finest grain and H2 is derived (†).

Band: H-grain rate ±2.0pp → 🟢↗ / 🟡→ / 🔴↘. Leadership metrics scored on direction of the company-reported delta vs the prior comparable print, never absolute level.

Derived figures (†): H2 = FY − H1, workings shown once per block. H2-25† organic (c.+0.5%) and PBT (≈£20.9m) were watch-flags until the 21 Jul 2026 H1 TU printed H1-26 figures corroborating the turn — now promoted from watch to confirmed.

Restatements & provenance (ʳ / basis): FY25 continuing-basis revenue £1,270.8m excludes exited Swiss MobilePro + a small N.Am unit; FY24 revenue comparator restated £1,317.0m → £1,289.5m and adj PBT restated to £39.1m continuing (£38.3m as originally reported — the figure used in the FY aggregate row, per each year's own first print).
