💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Quartix Technologies plc actually does

The box in the van

Quartix sells a little black box that gets fitted in work vans and lorries and phones home with where the vehicle is, how it's being driven and when it moved. Small businesses — plumbers, builders, delivery firms — pay a monthly subscription to see all of that on a map and in reports. Around 343,000 vehicles across 33,500 businesses are on the meter, in the UK, France, the USA, Italy, Spain and Germany. It also now sells dashcams as an add-on to the same customers.

How the money works

Nearly all the revenue (97%) is that monthly subscription — customers rarely buy the box, Quartix keeps owning it and takes it back or redeploys it when someone leaves. Once a box is in a van it just keeps billing, so the game is: fit more boxes, keep the customers you have, and nudge the price up a little each year. Founded 2001 in Wales by Andy Walters, who still runs it as Executive Chairman and is a major shareholder; listed on AIM.

Recent structural event

In 2025 the accounting watchdog (FRC) asked questions and the company changed how it books the boxes: they're now counted as long-life company kit written off over seven years (IAS 16 — spreading the cost of the box over its whole working life) rather than expensed over the first contract. Past figures were restated; cash was never affected.

The investment argument in one line: a sticky subscription machine compounding at 11% a year with net cash and a founder back at the wheel — the question is whether the new-customer engine is now slowing. As at Jul-26.

Turn methodology & restatement basis

Every Δ and arrow transcribes one script (turns.py, md5-verified re-run) with comparison bases hard-coded per metric type: ARR in-period growth, new subscriptions and new customers are flows compared like-period-to-like-period only (Q1-vs-Q1, H1-vs-H1 — never H1-vs-9M), because Q1 carries ~75% of annual price indexation; NRR is a rate scored on seasonally-matched Δ against the ±2.0pp/±5.0pp bands; revenue is scored on sequential HonH as the primary trigger plus the seasonally-matched YoY rate; the subscription base is a snapshot compared position-to-position at its own disclosure grain. Any negative sequential print fires 🔴↘ on level regardless of year-comp; comp-flags annotate, never suppress. Derivations: Q2† = H1 − Q1, Q3† = 9M − H1, Q4† = FY − 9M, H2† = FY − H1; second route H2-25† 1.8 = Q3† + Q4† ✓. Q2-26† cells rest on H1 parents now reported at the 27-Jul-26 interims (previously company estimates), but remain derived (⚑) until a reported sub-period corroborates. Provenance: ʳ = IAS-16 restatement of FY24/H1-25 applied in the FY25 accounts and interims note 9 (later audited figure governs the 08-Jul "c." estimates — EBITDA £6.9m→£7.2m, adj EBIT £4.6m→£4.9m, FCF £2.6m→£3.0m); FY23 new-subs and new-customers values are back-derived from FY24's stated growth rates (†); the Buy/Sell (rightmost populated) column is bold. Lost-unit fees reclassified out of revenue (−£0.3m H1-25) under the same policy — no cross-basis arithmetic crosses that boundary unflagged.
