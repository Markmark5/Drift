💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Quartix Technologies plc actually does

The box in the van

Quartix sells a little black box that gets fitted in work vans and lorries and phones home with where the vehicle is, how it's being driven and when it moved. Small businesses — plumbers, builders, delivery firms — pay a monthly subscription to see all of that on a map and in reports. Around 343,000 vehicles across 33,550 businesses are on the meter, in the UK, France, the USA, Italy, Spain and Germany. It also sells dashcams as an add-on to the same customers.

How the money works

Nearly all the revenue (98% in the latest half) is that monthly subscription — customers rarely buy the box, Quartix keeps owning it and takes it back or redeploys it when someone leaves. Once a box is in a van it just keeps billing, so the game is: fit more boxes, keep the customers you have, and nudge the price up a little each year. Founded 2001 in Wales by Andy Walters, who still runs it as Executive Chairman and holds 22%; listed on AIM.

Recent structural event

In 2025 the accounting watchdog asked questions and the company changed how it books the boxes: they're now counted as long-life company kit written off over seven years rather than expensed over the first contract. Past figures were restated; cash was never affected.

The investment argument in one line: a sticky subscription machine compounding at 11% a year with net cash and a founder back at the wheel — the question is whether the new-customer engine is now slowing. As at Jul-26.

Turn methodology & restatement basis



Comparison basis per rank. R1, R2 and R3 are flows compared like-period to like-period (Q1 against Q1, Q2† against Q2†, H1 against H1, FY against FY), because Q1 carries roughly 75% of the annual price indexation and a sequential read would fire on seasonality alone. R4 is a rate scored on the change in position since the last print, in percentage points. R5 is a half-year flow displayed on the same-period-last-year comparison and scored on the banded change in that growth rate.


Display basis. Cells show the comparison named in the row label; the unused comparison is computed and retained here, never displayed. Raw half-on-half revenue for the Buy/Sell period is +5.4%. Quarter-on-quarter new subscriptions for Q2-26 is −12%.


Stated unit per rank. R1 £m · R2 units · R3 customers · R4 % · R5 £m. Each is the same string in the section bar, the row label and the rendered values.


Derivations. Q2† = H1 − Q1 · Q3† = 9M − H1 · Q4† = FY − 9M · H2† = FY − H1. Second route: H2-25† 1.8 = Q3† + Q4† ✓. All derived cells carry † and the derived-only watch-flag ⚑ where they fire.


Restatement and provenance. ʳ marks the IAS 16 restatement of FY24 and H1-25 applied in the FY25 accounts and repeated in interims note 9. Later audited figures govern the 08-Jul-26 estimates: EBITDA £6.9m → £7.2m, adjusted EBIT £4.6m → £4.9m, FCF £2.6m → £3.0m. FY23 new subscriptions and new customers are back-derived from FY24's stated growth rates (†); H1-24 new subscriptions back-derived from H1-25's stated +7% (†).
