💡 Idiot's guide to the company — What The Alumasc Group plc actually does

Alumasc makes the unglamorous parts of a building that stop water ruining it. Based in Burton Latimer, Northamptonshire; listed on AIM. It manufactures and sells — it does not install, and hasn't since 2022.

Water Management — about 44p in every sales pound

Drainage. Gutters and downpipes (ARP, Alumasc Rainwater), floor gullies and roof outlets (Wade), and the heavy engineered end — access covers and slot drains sunk into airport runways and ports (Gatic). The big export jobs are lumpy: one Hong Kong airport contract worth over £7m swung two years of results by itself. Who controls the timing? The customer. Alumasc ships when they call it off, and they call it off when they're ready.

Building Envelope — about 39p

Flat roofing systems, waterproofing membranes, green roofs and solar-ready roofs, sold as Alumasc Roofing. It wins by getting specified: an architect writes the product into the drawings, and the contractor then has to buy it.

Housebuilding Products — about 17p

Timloc. Loft hatches, roof vents, cavity trays — small plastic parts, thousands of them, next-day to builders' merchants. Smallest division, best margin by a distance (26%), and the only one that grew through the worst housing market since Covid.

Why any of it works

Roughly 80% of sales are pulled through by building regulations rather than chosen on price. Someone has to fit a compliant roof vent; the argument is about which one, not whether.

Recent changes worth knowing

Sold Levolux (loss-making solar shading) in 2022. Bought ARP rainwater goods for up to £10m in December 2023. In 2026 the chief executive of 23 years retired, his successor lasted fifteen weeks before being suspended pending an investigation into her conduct, and the Chair is now running the company.

The argument for owning it: a regulation-protected niche manufacturer with a 26%-margin jewel in the middle of it and almost no debt, priced for a construction downturn it has so far outgrown.

Turn methodology & restatement basis

All Δ, rates, rate-deltas and arrows are transcribed from one script, with the comparison basis hard-coded per metric type. Nothing on this page was computed by eye.

Snapshot metrics (Rank 1) compare position-to-position at their own disclosure grain — never against a period flow. Flow metrics (Ranks 4, 5) compare like-period to like-period only: H1 against H1, H2 against H2, never H1 against a full year. Rate metrics (Ranks 2, 3) use the seasonally matched pp change. Turn arrows fire on Δ(YoY rate) against the prior seasonally matched period at a band of ±2.0pp, constant within this run: 🟢↗ above, 🔴↘ below, 🟡→ within.

Why there is no quarter grain. ALU issues Q1 updates (the October AGM statements) and, from Apr-2026, a Q3 update — but no document has ever printed a quantified quarterly revenue or profit level. The Q1/AGM updates are qualitative throughout; the single Q3 TU prints growth rates only (+2% YoY, monthly run 8% above H1). 9M is never printed, so Q2† = H1 − Q1 and Q4† = FY − 9M are not derivable. The hard gate is satisfied by demonstrated non-derivability, and the finest available sub-period — H2† = FY − H1 — is computed and scored throughout.

Derived cells. Every H2 figure on this page is derived (†) and every H2 Turn is therefore a watch flag until a printed sub-period corroborates it — parent tie-out is arithmetic, not corroboration. One partial exception: the Apr-26 Q3 TU prints Q3 FY26 revenue at +2% year-on-year, which does independently corroborate the direction of the H2 FY26† revenue read (+1.1%). No printed figure corroborates any H2 profit turn.

Cross-verification of the derived halves. Margin was derived independently as (FY operating profit − H1 operating profit) ÷ derived H2 revenue, then checked against the printed H1 margins: tie-outs of −0.04, −0.07, −0.08, +0.01 and +0.03pp across H1 FY22 to H1 FY26. All H1 operating profit inputs (£6.5m, £6.0m, £6.7m, £8.1m, £4.5m) are printed figures, not derived — so each H2 is a single-step derivation.

Restatements and provenance. ʳ FY24 restated in the FY25 results (2 Sep 2025): Group margin 14.1%→14.3%, operating profit £14.2m→£14.4m, Water Mgmt 15.8%→15.9%, Building Envelope 12.3%→12.4%, Housebuilding 25.3%→25.5%.
