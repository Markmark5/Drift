💡 Idiot's guide to the companies — What the UK's five largest listed housebuilders actually do
🔵 Barratt Redrow (BTRW) · June FY

The UK's biggest housebuilder by volume, formed in August 2024 when Barratt Developments bought Redrow for ~£2.5bn. Builds and sells private and affordable homes across England, Scotland and Wales under three brands — Barratt Homes (mass-market), David Wilson Homes (premium) and Redrow (family/premium). Revenue comes almost entirely from selling completed homes; it doesn't rent. Based in London; FY ends June. Medium-term target: 22,000 homes a year. ᵇ Pre-FY25 figures are Barratt standalone.

🟣 Persimmon (PSN) · December FY

Second-largest, focused on affordable and mid-market homes, with a vertically integrated model (owns Space4 timber-frame factory and other supply-chain units) that keeps build costs below peers. Three brands: Persimmon Homes, Charles Church (premium) and Westbury Partnerships (affordable). York-based; below-average ASP is a deliberate positioning choice, not a weakness. Consistently industry-leading margins. FY ends December.

🟢 Taylor Wimpey (TW) · December FY

Volume housebuilder with 22 UK regional businesses plus a Spanish operation. Builds mainly private homes; known for large strategic landbank (c.132k plots). Revenue split roughly 95% UK housing. Has a differentiated Ordinary Dividend Policy (distribution % of net assets). Gerrards Cross-based; FY ends December. Has been running a significant cladding fire-safety remediation programme (£225m+ exceptional in FY25).

🟡 Berkeley Group (BKG) · April FY ᵍ

The outlier. Exclusively London, Birmingham and South-East brownfield regeneration — complex urban apartment schemes that take 5–10 years from land purchase to completion. ASP roughly 2–3× the sector average. Does not report reservations per outlet in the standard form; guides and reports via £m "cash due on forward sales" and multi-year profit targets. Building its own Build-to-Rent platform (Berkeley Living). FY ends April — columns are ~2–4 months out of calendar phase with the other four.

🔵 Bellway (BWY) · July FY

National volume builder, traditionally strong in the North and Midlands. July year-end means its H1 runs August–January and its H2 runs February–July — the opposite seasonal shape to PSN/TW. Known for disciplined land buying and conservative guidance. Running a £150m share buyback launched Oct 2025 while volume recovers post-trough.

Investment argument in one line: the sector is a pure play on UK mortgage affordability and planning reform — when rates fall and outlets open, margins recover fast; the question is timing, not direction. As at Jul 2026.
