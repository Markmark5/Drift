💡 Idiot's guide to the company — What GlobalData plc actually does

GlobalData is a London-based subscription business that sells industry intelligence — deep data, forecasts and expert analysis on sectors like pharmaceuticals, consumer, tech and financial services. Think of it as a library of hard-to-find market facts that big companies pay a yearly fee to keep dipping into, increasingly served up through an AI layer (its "AI Hub" and research assistant "Ava," now plugged into Microsoft Copilot).

How the money works

Almost all revenue is recurring subscriptions — customers renew every year, which is why the "renewal rate" and the "contracted forward revenue" (orders already booked for future months) matter more than any single sale. The business runs as two arms: a larger Non-Healthcare side and a Healthcare side that serves drug and life-sciences firms.

Who's in charge

Founder-CEO Mike Danson owns ~60% of the shares — he controls the company outright, so shareholder votes are effectively his to decide. In mid-2024 he sold 40% of the Healthcare arm to private-equity house Inflexion, and the group runs the two divisions' balance sheets separately.

Recent structural events

The company moved from AIM to the Main Market of the London Stock Exchange in March 2026, fended off two takeover approaches in mid-2025, and has been buying small "bolt-on" businesses while returning cash to shareholders via buybacks and tender offers.

The pitch: a sticky, cash-generative data subscription machine whose profit margin dipped while it invested — buy it for the recovery, if growth actually re-accelerates.
