💡Idiot’s guide to the company — What SysGroup actually does

What it is

SysGroup is a managed IT services provider — an outsourced IT department for mid-sized British firms. Fewer than 100 people now. It runs customers’ systems, keeps them secure, and increasingly tells them what to buy in the first place.

Where it’s based

AIM-listed, with offices in Edinburgh, London, Manchester, Pinewood and Newport. Liverpool was the head office from listing until 2026. Year-end 31 March.

How it makes money

Managed IT Services — around 86% of sales. Monthly contracts to host, run and defend a customer’s systems, plus consulting, security advice and project work. Sticky, and the company sets the price. Cybersecurity is 46% of all group sales and is the part growing.

Value Added Resale — the other 14%. Buying hardware and software licences from vendors and selling them on with installation. Who controls the price. The customer’s buying cycle sets the timing, so it swings hard — one half it doubles, the next it halves. It earns 37p per pound of sales against 50p on the contract side.

What’s changed recently

Heejae Chae bought 14% of the company and became Executive Chairman in June 2023, replacing a nine-year CEO. He redirected the firm towards AI — both as something to sell and as a way to cut its own cost of answering support tickets. Two bad years followed, then a recovery. Bought Crossword Cybersecurity’s consulting arm in late 2024 and Saxis in late 2025. Craig Ormesher was made permanent CFO in November 2026 after four months as interim. The bank facility now runs to April 2032.

The bet, in one line: buy it if AI genuinely lowers what it costs to serve a customer, so the same revenue drops more profit — avoid it if the contracts keep quietly shrinking and only bought-in revenue covers the gap.

Turn methodology & restatement basis

Comparison basis. R1–R4 are flow metrics on an H1/H2† spine, displaying the year-on-year move and scoring the banded change in that rate between adjacent half-columns, band ±2.0pp. R5 is a rate metric scoring the pp move against the same period a year earlier, band ±1.5pp stated for this ticker and not inherited from the flow band.

Display basis. Year-on-year renders in the cell; the sequential comparison is computed and retained here, never displayed. No pp values or rate labels appear in the grid — the emoji alone carries the score.

Rule applied. All five ranks render under the default H1/H2 rule. None is reduced-grain; H2 is derivable in every complete year of the record. No qualitative ladder. Router: H-reporter, Rule Set H — no trading update in this record has ever carried quarterly financials.

Units. R1–R4 carry £’000 at the section bar, the row label and the rendered magnitude; R5 carries % and no currency. All five figures come from the face of the income statement or the segmental note.

Spine. Nine half-columns, H1 FY23 to H1 FY27, rolled one column at this read. Held as comparators only and never rendered: H1 FY22, H2 FY22 and the FY22 aggregate, which exist so the first rendered column has both a year-ago base and a prior growth rate to be scored against.

Zero-crossing guard: checked, not fired. Cadence proved before each blank. The pattern under the current chairman, updated. This read is the first clear break in that pattern: cash conversion is back at both grains, a prior withheld figure has been backfilled, a new reconciliation is printed, and a definition change is disclosed and reconciled rather than left for the reader to find.
