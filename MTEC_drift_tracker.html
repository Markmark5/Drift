💡 Idiot's guide to the company — What Made Tech actually does
What they sell

Made Tech builds and runs the digital plumbing of British government — the websites you use to renew something, the case systems caseworkers type into, the data platforms behind them. Founded 2008, floated on AIM in 2021.

Where they are

London head office, with people in Manchester and Bristol and a lot of the work done sitting inside client buildings.

How the money arrives

They sell their people's time. Departments buy through government purchasing frameworks, sign up for a few years, and pay largely by the day. Two consequences: growth needs bodies, and every contract eventually runs out and has to be won again. That second one is why the order book matters more here than the sales line.

Their own staff cost less than hired-in contractors, so the more work their employees do rather than freelancers, the more of each pound survives to profit. That single swap is most of the margin story of the last two years.

Recent changes worth knowing

A new finance chief arrived March 2026 — the second in three years. The government's June 2025 Spending Review released department money from April 2026, which is fresh demand arriving just after a thin year for orders.

The investment argument in one line: a re-rated services business whose profits are rising much faster than its sales, where the only real question is whether the order book refills fast enough to keep it going.

Turn methodology & restatement basis

    Turn methodology. Every Δ and arrow is computed in one script with the comparison basis hard-coded per metric type. Sales Bookings and Revenue are flows, compared like-period-to-like-period only (H1-vs-H1, FY-vs-FY) — the H1→FY step within a year is a seasonal build, not a scored turn. Contracted Backlog and Net Cash are snapshots, compared position-to-position at their own disclosure grain. Adjusted EBITDA margin is a rate, seasonally matched, banded at ±2.0pp (🟢 above / 🟡 within / 🔴 below). The spine is H1 / H2†: MTEC prints H1 and FY only, so every H2 cell is derived as FY − H1 (both inputs exact, same basis) and carries †. No printed sub-period exists to corroborate an H2, so per the derived-only rule an H2 turn is a watch-flag until an H1 print confirms the direction — no condition in this build fires on an H2 cell alone. FY totals sit on the muted aggregate rows, transcribed from company prints, never summed from cells, never scored. H1 FY22 comparatives (revenue £11.7m, bookings £26.5m, backlog £31.3m, cash £11.1m, margin 10.1%) are taken from the Feb-23 H1 results table. Company-rate note: MTEC states H1 FY23 bookings +24%; the printed levels (£32.6m / £26.5m) compute to +23.0% — input rounding, levels govern. Rank 3 derivation basis: H2 margins are derived as (FY Adjusted EBITDA − H1 Adjusted EBITDA) ÷ H2 revenue†, with FY25 taken at the audited £3.5m (the Jun-25 TU's c.£3.4m is superseded — later governs earlier) and FY22 at the exact £2,649k from the FY22 statements. H1 FY22 Adjusted EBITDA is available only as narrative £1.2m, so H2 FY22† mixes an exact full year with a rounded half — a precision-basis break, marked ◊ and carried unscored (it is the first period in the series and has no comparator regardless). Every other H2 margin derives from £0.1m-rounded inputs whose implied uncertainty (±0.5–0.9pp) sits inside the ±2.0pp band, so no further ◊ applies. Basis change not affecting a rank: Sep-25 restated FY24 gross profit and gross margin (£14.0m/36.3% to £13.2m/34.2%) to include the full cost of delivery consultants; gross margin is not a ranked metric and Adjusted EBITDA margin is unaffected. Flags: r restated — the FY24 backlog rebase (−£5.0m for prior-period contract expiries, per the Feb-25 footnote) applies to both FY24 halves: FY24 £65.6m as printed becomes £60.6m restated, and H1 FY24 £61.3m as printed becomes £56.3m restated (Feb-25 prints the £56.3m comparative). Cells carry the figure as printed at the time, with each comparison run on a single consistent basis: FY24-vs-FY23 uses printed-vs-printed (£65.6m vs £67.9m, −3.4%, matching the company's "only 3% down"), while H1 FY25 and FY25 are compared to the restated £56.3m / £60.6m (+£20.2m; +52.1%, matching the company's stated 44% and 52%). No comparison mixes the two bases. H1 FY23 EBITDA margin carried at the Feb-24 restated 2.5% (originally printed 2.4%), Δ −7.6pp vs the H1 FY22 10.1% seasonal comp; g gross figure (from Dec-25 the company splits gross vs net bookings — H1 FY26 gross £13.4m / net £9.9m after £3.5m lapsed; the grid uses gross throughout for like-for-like). Book-to-bill† = gross Sales Bookings ÷ revenue, same period — a derived watch ratio, never scored (both inputs already scored separately); >1x funds future growth, <1x consumes backlog; half-by-half in the thin row (H2 FY26 ≥1.32x = £41m H2-td ÷ £31.1m†), full-year on the R1 aggregate row (FY26 ≥0.92x = £54m YTD ÷ £58.9m); both FY26 figures are floors since post-15-Apr bookings are unprinted. y YTD figure at 15 Apr 2026 (contract-award RNS, blue column) — mid-period, not a like-period comp; annotation only, never scored. ❌ = verified withhold against established cadence.
