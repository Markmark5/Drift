💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Bango plc actually does

🎟️ What it is

Bango is the meter sitting between a phone bill and everything bought on it. When someone taps "pay" in an app store or a streaming service and the charge lands on their mobile bill instead of a card, Bango's plumbing is usually what made that work and what counted the money.

📍 Where it's based

Cambridge, UK. Listed on AIM. Run through partnerships worldwide rather than offices in every country it touches.

💰 How the money comes in — two taps

Payments (the old tap, being closed off on purpose): a slice of every purchase flowing through phone billing. Shrinking deliberately as the cheap, low-margin routes get switched off.

Subscriptions (the tap they're opening wide): software sold to big telecoms and retailers — the DVM product — letting them bundle streaming and music into their own deals and keep track of who's entitled to what. Priced like software, so far more of each sale survives to the bottom.

🕹️ Who's in control

An independent Chair and a freshly appointed Audit Chair now sit over the executive team. The founders are still around, but the grown-ups have moved in.

🏗️ Recent big change

A pricey inherited platform from the Docomo Digital deal was mostly torn out through FY24–FY25 along with 55 jobs, which is what cleared the path for the current profit recovery.

In one line: a small British toll-booth on mobile spending, closing the cheap roads and widening the profitable ones.

Turn methodology & restatement basis

Comparison bases. Every delta and arrow above was computed in one script from printed values, with the basis hard-coded per metric type. SEQUENTIAL = versus the period immediately before. YEAR-ON-YEAR = versus the same period one year earlier (H1-vs-H1, H2-vs-H2). RATE CHANGE (Δrate) = the change in the year-on-year figure between consecutive like periods, measured in pp and banded. Rank 1 (snapshot) is scored on its year-add under the operator override recorded in the notes drawer; Rank 2 (rate metric) is scored on the year-on-year pp move directly, banded ±5.0pp; Ranks 3–5 (flow) are scored on the banded Δrate at ±2.0pp. Display basis: flow rows display the year-on-year figure beside the value, with the Δrate as the scored element carried by the emoji alone; the raw sequential move is computed in script and retained here, never displayed in cells. 🍺 IDIOT'S GUIDE TO THE ADJUSTED EBITDA BRIDGE — why profit went up ($6.7m → $9.0m)
🧹 The great clear-out+n/qLast year they cut 55 jobs and ripped out the inherited Docomo plumbing they'd been paying to keep running alongside their own. Those bills simply don't arrive any more, so this half started the race with a head start. Biggest single reason by a distance.
💎 Fussier about customers+n/qThey're walking away from the cheap phone-billing routes where almost nothing survived to the bottom, and leaning on software deals where most of the sale sticks. Sell roughly the same, keep a much fatter slice.
🌱 Money out the door chasing tomorrow−n/qThey didn't pocket every penny saved — a chunk went straight back out chasing the next batch of telecom customers. It eats into this half's number, but it's the sort of spending you want to see.
So in one sentence: the old business costs far less to run and every sale keeps more of itself, minus a slice reinvested — a $2.3m jump to at least $9.0m. Profit growth speeds up again when: they start selling more, not just spending less. The savings are now fully banked, which is exactly why the growth RATE fell from 68% to 34% even as the cash pile grew.
