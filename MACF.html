💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Macfarlane Group actually does

What it is

A protective-packaging business: it sells boxes, bubble wrap, tape, foam and fitted packaging to over 20,000 business customers, so their goods arrive unbroken.

Where it's based

Glasgow, Scotland — about 42 sites across the UK plus Ireland, Germany and the Netherlands. Listed in London since 1973.

How it makes money

Two arms. Packaging Distribution (about three-quarters of sales) buys packaging from around 2,000 suppliers and delivers it to customers from 26 regional depots — a wholesaler with vans and advice. Manufacturing Operations designs and makes protective packaging itself, mainly foam and corrugate for high-value, fragile kit like aerospace and medical equipment.

Who controls the price. Suppliers set raw-material costs; Macfarlane earns its keep in the gap between buying price and selling price, so its profit lives and dies on how well it passes cost moves on to customers.

What's changed recently

Years of buying small rivals swelled the group. In 2025 a fatal accident shut a factory at Pitreavie, its newest purchase, and profit fell hard; 2026 is billed as the recovery year. A June 2026 insurance deal took the old staff pension scheme off the group's risk list.

The bet, in one line: a steady boxes-and-vans compounder that stumbled badly in 2025 and is now priced on whether the repair job is real.
