💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Audioboom Group plc actually does

What it is

Audioboom sells the advertising slots inside other people's podcasts and keeps a cut. It is the fifth-largest podcast publisher in the United States, reaching 58 million listeners a month.

Where it's based

Listed in London on AIM, run from London and New York, with roughly 92% of the money coming from the United States. It reports in US dollars.

How it makes money

Premium, about half the money: a well-known host reads an ad out loud in their own voice. Audioboom sells the slot, collects the cash and hands most of it back to the podcaster.

Showcase, about four dollars in ten and the fastest-growing: software drops ads into episodes automatically, so the same episode carries different ads for different listeners. Nobody records anything. Audioboom keeps more of each dollar here.

Sonic, roughly a dollar in ten: a small team planning podcast campaigns directly for a handful of big brands. It has been shrinking.

Who controls the price. Advertisers do, mostly. Audioboom sets rates but demand swings with the wider advertising market — in 2023 it fell away and took the profits with it. The company's own lever is which shows it signs and what it promises them.

What's changed recently

Bought Adelicious, a UK podcast network, in July 2025, adding about 20 million monthly listens. Ran a strategic review from October 2025 after press speculation about a takeover; the review ended in June 2026 with the board rejecting the offers and staying independent. A $10m credit line, agreed in principle in July 2026, was confirmed in October 2026.

The catch worth knowing. To sign the biggest podcasts, Audioboom often promises the creator a minimum payment whether or not the ads sell. When advertising was weak in 2023 those promises turned into losses. Both of the bad ones have now expired — but the mechanism reappeared, smaller, in the first half of 2026.

The bet, in one line: a sales business whose costs barely move, so every extra advertising dollar drops mostly to profit — as long as the promises made to creators stay smaller than the money the ads bring in.

Stable facts, as at 15 July 2026; credit-facility status updated 14 October 2026.
