💡 Idiot’s guide to the company — What Focusrite plc actually does

What they make

Focusrite builds the kit that turns a spare bedroom into a recording studio, and the kit that fills a stadium with sound. Head office is in High Wycombe, west of London. Listed on AIM since 2014; the man who founded it in 1989 is still on the board.

The two halves

Content Creation is about 72% of sales — things a musician buys for themselves. The Scarlett recording box is the one nearly everybody starts with, and it is the most-used device of its kind anywhere. Alongside it sit studio speakers (ADAM), synthesisers (Novation, Sequential, Oberheim) and sound-editing software (Sonnox). Nearly all of it is built by hired factories in China and Malaysia, then sold to distributors and shops — and, more and more, straight off Focusrite’s own website. That direct channel was 12% of the division in the last six months and pays better, because nobody in the middle takes a cut.

Audio Reproduction is the other 28% — speaker systems for venues, festivals and permanent installations, mostly under the Martin Audio name. Bigger orders, longer lead times, built largely in Britain, sold through the firms that design and fit out venues. That means management can see the order book months ahead.

Who controls what

The brands and the designs are owned outright. The factories are not. So when American import taxes landed in 2025, the levers available were raising prices and shifting production between countries — and both were pulled.

What changed lately

The financial year end moved from August to February, so the newest accounts cover an odd 18-month stretch that does not line up with the older ones. The auditor changed from KPMG to EY. Two purchases bedded in (Sonnox 2022, Sheriff 2023). The synthesiser arm, Sequential, has now been written down twice.

The argument in one line: a set of brands musicians genuinely love, throwing off cash and finally widening margins — the open question is whether sales can grow again once the tariff distortions wash through.

🔒 LOCKED PARAMETER — BAND CLASSIFICATION (reproduce verbatim; do not re-derive)

TUNE — band classification, v4.7 build (band first set under v3.15 run 1). Locked at operator instruction, 28 July 2026. This is a declared run parameter, not a derived value. It is not to be recomputed, “corrected” to a single band, or reconciled against the spec’s ±2.0pp default during any audit stage. Stage C verifies arrows against this classification, not against ±2.0pp universally.

Two-tier band, constant per metric type, applied to the entire history including periods that argue against the current read:

  • Growth-rate (Δ of a year-on-year % growth rate) → ±2.0pp. Rank 1 Content Creation revenue · Rank 2 Audio Reproduction revenue · Rank 4 Group revenue · Rank 5 Adjusted EBITDA.
  • Level-rate (a ratio reported as a level; the level-difference is the rate delta — no double-differencing) → ±1.0pp. Rank 3 Gross margin % · (informational) Adjusted EBITDA margin %.
  • Snapshot (a position at a date) → no band. Net debt.
  • Soft (qualitative-only) → n/a. End-user registrations / channel-stock — §4 Drift only, unscored since the v4.7 build.

Why ±1.0pp for level-rate. The ±2.0pp default is calibrated to growth rates, whose Δrate in this run spans −41.2pp to +46.8pp. TUNE’s gross margin has a total lifetime range of 4.7pp across nine halves (43.2%–47.9%); a ±2.0pp band would require the metric to travel roughly 43% of its entire four-year range before registering, and it has done so only twice, both off one-offs.

Backtest that justifies it (the only non-circular test). At ±1.0pp gross margin flags 🔴 at Feb-24 — the half the FY24 profit collapse began — one half earlier than at ±2.0pp. A band that lengthens the metric’s historical lead is the better band.

Anti-tuning record. The change weakens the deterioration read it was chosen under: it hands Rank 3 two consecutive 🟢 and activates a green-shoot mirror condition. Cells changed: 3, all Rank 3. Growth-rate ranks: 0 arrows changed.

🔒 LOCKED PARAMETER — §2 GRID AGGREGATE ROW (reproduce verbatim; do not strip)

Locked at operator instruction, 28 July 2026. Adopted as spec from v3.16; retained under v4.7. v3.15 §2 specified one value row per rank. This build renders a second row. It is intentional and is not to be removed, flattened, or “restored to spec” during any audit stage.

Each rank block renders: section bar → period strip → half value row (9 cells) → aggregate row, where each fiscal year’s halves merge into one cell of identical total width (colspan 2; colspan 3 for the 18-month transition band). Where the company printed a non-standard aggregate it renders as a second sub-span cell beneath only the columns it covers (pro-forma 12m to Feb-26 → colspan 2 over Aug-25 + Feb-26, preceded by a colspan 7 spacer).

Aggregate cells stay all-white per the locked no-tint rule, distinguished by a 2px top rule and muted type only. Aggregate values are transcriptions of company-printed full-period figures — they are not summed from the half cells and are never scored. Turn arrows live on the half row only, because the aggregate never governs.

Rationale. An H-grain reporter publishes H1 and FY. H2 exists only by subtraction, so a second-half slowdown is invisible in the printed record. Stacking the derived halves above the printed total makes the shape of the year legible without arithmetic — serving the spec’s stated principle that “the aggregate can hide the turn.”
