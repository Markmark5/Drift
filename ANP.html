💡 Idiot’s guide to the company — What Anpario plc actually does

What it is

Anpario makes natural feed additives — powders, liquids and boluses that farmers mix into animal feed or drinking water to keep livestock healthy without reaching for antibiotics. It is listed on London’s AIM market as ANP.

Where it is based

Head office and the main factory sit at Manton Wood near Worksop in Nottinghamshire, England. A second plant in Wisconsin, USA arrived with the Bio-Vet purchase. Sales companies of its own operate in places including China, Brazil, Vietnam and, since 2025, Panama.

How the money comes in

One kind of product, sold two ways: through local distributors, or straight to farms and feed mills by Anpario’s own people on the ground. The push has been steadily towards the second, because selling direct earns more per bag. Sales are counted in four regions — the Americas, Asia, Europe, and India / Middle East / Africa. The goods are branded: Orego-Stim (oregano oil), pHorce (an acid blend), Anpro (mops up mould poisons in grain), Mastercube (holds feed pellets together), Optomega Algae (omega-3), plus Bio-Vet’s QuadriCal calcium boluses and its Generator probiotic.

Who runs it

Richard Edwards has been Chief Executive throughout this record; Marc Wilson is Group Finance Director; Matthew Robinson took the chair in June 2023 from Kate Allum. Shore Capital is nominated adviser and broker, having replaced Peel Hunt in 2023.

What changed recently

Bio-Vet Inc. of Wisconsin was bought on 30 September 2024 for £5.8m all in. It brought a US dairy business, a second factory and £6.7m of sales in 2025. A £3.0m programme of buying back its own shares began in 2026. There is no debt.

The argument, in one line: a small British maker of natural feed additives with no borrowings and a widening range, betting that the world’s farmers keep being pushed away from antibiotics and towards what it sells.

The acid-based products — a fifth of everything sold — got pricier to make. Prices went up to match, so profit per pound sold is holding level with the back half of last year rather than climbing again.
So in one sentence: fresh orders in the Gulf and steady American cows are pushing profit up, while dearer freight and dearer acid are quietly stopping profit-per-pound from improving any further.
Profit goes back up harder when: the price rises stick without customers walking, freight settles down, and the Gulf recovery lasts longer than one good quarter.
