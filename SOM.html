💡Idiot's guide to the company — What Somero Enterprises actually does

What it is

Somero makes the machines that lay flat concrete floors — big laser-guided screeds that ride over wet concrete and level it to within a few millimetres. If you've walked through a warehouse, a data centre or a car factory, you've probably walked on a Somero floor.

Where it's based

Fort Myers, Florida, with a factory in Houghton, Michigan. Listed on AIM in London, reports in US dollars.

How it makes money

Selling machines (about 60% of revenue — the big "Boomed" screeds are the largest single line at ~39%), plus parts, service and training on the installed fleet (~20%), plus smaller lines like remanufactured units and 3D profiling systems. Around three-quarters of sales are in North America. Who controls the price. Somero — it's the dominant maker with its own patents. But it can only sell when contractors have floors to pour, and that depends on interest rates, project starts and labour availability, none of which it controls.

What's changed recently

Three years of falling sales (2023–25) as US non-residential building slowed. Whole leadership changed: new CEO (Tim Averkamp, 2025), new Chair, new COO. Dividend cut hard. New cheaper products launched (Hammerhead, S-15EZ). July 2026: first guide raise in the whole period.

The bet, in one line: if US contractors start pouring floors again, Somero is the only game in town — and its costs have already been cut to fit a smaller world.
